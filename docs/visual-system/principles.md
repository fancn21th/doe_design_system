# DOE Visual Principles

## Character

DOE product surfaces are industrial, precise, analytical, and engineering
oriented. They help users inspect experimental configuration, execution state,
measurements, and evidence; they are not marketing surfaces.

## Data First

Make the information needed for a decision easy to scan, compare, and verify.
Structure, labels, units, status, and evidence take priority over decoration.

## Structure Before Shadow

Create hierarchy with grouping, spacing, alignment, borders, and typography
before introducing shadow. A shadow is reserved for a meaningful elevation or
layering relationship; it is not the default way to make a DOE surface feel
important.

## Dense but Readable

Prefer compact layouts, efficient tables, and concise controls. Preserve clear
hierarchy, adequate hit targets, visible grouping, and local scrolling for wide
data. Density must not make a value, state, or action ambiguous.

## Semantic, Not Decorative

Use colour, emphasis, and motion to communicate status, warnings, selection,
measurement meaning, and hierarchy. Do not add them as ornament. A neutral
surface is the default; stronger treatment needs a stated semantic reason.

## No Page-Local Visual Invention

Application composition may arrange documented assets responsively, but it may
not introduce an unrecorded colour, typography hierarchy, card treatment,
spacing rhythm, or control variant. A repeated need belongs in the design
system; a domain-specific need belongs in that domain asset's contract.

## Visual Decision Framework

When visual goals compete, optimise in this order:

1. Information clarity;
2. Domain meaning and evidence integrity;
3. Consistency with documented tokens, Patterns, and Domain assets;
4. Visual refinement;
5. Individual preference.

A request for stronger emphasis, novelty, or decoration must not reduce the
first three. For example, make a KPI more discoverable with documented
hierarchy and semantic emphasis before increasing its scale or adding a
marketing-style treatment.

## Evidence Over Implication

Do not turn planned conditions into actual observations, descriptive evidence
into causal conclusions, or absent data into a passing state. The visual system
must preserve the certainty and provenance supplied by the domain contract.

## Consistency Through Reuse

Reuse documented tokens, patterns, and domain assets. A visual difference must
express a DOE semantic difference, not an isolated implementation preference.
